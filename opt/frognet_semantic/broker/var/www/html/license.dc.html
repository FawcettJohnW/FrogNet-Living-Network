§Why I ask at all

Why there's a form.

I want you to have this. The form isn't a gate to keep you out or a funnel to sell you something — it exists so I can steer FrogNet coherently while it becomes a standard, and to meet the law I'm bound by. Once it's done, the free license is genuinely free.

Stewardship

Keeping it coherent

FrogNet is meant to become the foundation of an open standard — a real RFC, with Magnum Croakus as its seed. I've watched projects I admired fracture into incompatible dialects, or their authors burn out under design-by-committee, before the ground was solid. Knowing who's building on it lets me steward one coherent fabric until the specification and its foundation can carry that weight on their own. To be clear about what I'm stewarding: the protocol is the open part — the public contract anyone implements — and the engine is my reference implementation, kept closed only so a half-finished internal API doesn't harden into a compatibility promise before the spec freezes.

The law

I'm legally required to

FrogNet is U.S.-origin software, so U.S. export-control law obliges me to know who's receiving it and where. That's the country field and the three attestations — not bureaucracy I invented, obligations I have to meet before I can hand you the distribution.
