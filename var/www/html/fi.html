Why Local-First Everything

The cloud is just someone else’s computers on someone else’s power.

“The cloud” is not magic. It’s a cluster of power-hungry data centers, run by people you don’t control, reachable over networks you don’t own. When the Internet fails or a provider changes terms, your “smart” world can go dark. Fawcett Innovations, LLC starts from a different assumption: critical networks and AI must run under your control, on hardware you own, with the cloud as a useful extra – not a dependency.

FrogNet Platform

A portable, self-forming intranet you actually own.

FrogNet from Fawcett Innovations, LLC is a stand-alone TCP/IP Class C network you can spin up anywhere. It gives you local DNS, DHCP, routing, dashboards, media and data services – all on a small Linux host and a router, no upstream connection required.

Core capabilities
  • Sovereign intranets: Each FrogNet runs its own DHCP, DNS, and routing, so local devices keep working even when the ISP is down.
  • Network of networks: Join 2, 4, or 20 FrogNets into a single “internet-of-FrogNets” where every site has its own subnet and hostname space.
  • Smart segmentation: Keep smart home gadgets on one network and sensitive devices on another – with a true air gap between them.
  • Commodity hardware: Raspberry Pi, Intel NUCs, retired laptops, off-the-shelf routers – all orchestrated by the FrogNet host stack.
  • Self-hosted services: Run media servers, file shares, Home Assistant, Ghost, Jami, Firefly III, PhotoPrism, and more entirely on your network.

Keep calm when the ISP isn’t

When the Internet drops, your FrogNet keeps right on working. Cameras, dashboards, files, and local chat stay online. If you later connect an upstream link, all your FrogNets can share it automatically.

Built by network engineers

Under the hood, FrogNet is mostly Bash and well-understood Linux tools like dnsmasq, NetworkManager, and ip. It automates what an expert would do by hand – without turning your network into a black box.

Real-world topology

From single-node to roaming mesh

FrogNets don’t care if your links are fiber, Wi-Fi, or a pair of USB–Ethernet adapters across a field. If Linux can see it as an interface and you can point an ip route at it, FrogNet can incorporate it.

  • Spin up a single FrogNet for your home or cabin – a full intranet in a shoebox.
  • Drop additional FrogNets into outbuildings, remote cameras, or vehicles; they auto-discover and sync.
  • Build “snake” and “hub-and-spoke” topologies to stitch together neighborhoods, farms, or incident scenes.
  • Switch or add backhauls in minutes – plug in a SAT modem, LTE hotspot, microwave link, or radio TNC and update ip routes.
BullFrog Long Distance Communication™

A breakthrough in ultra-low-bandwidth, low-latency radio networking.

When you only have a crackly VHF link, a shared SAT hotspot, or a borrowed microwave hop, traditional IP and HTTP are painfully inefficient. BullFrog Long Distance Communication™ (BullFrog LDC) is Fawcett Innovations, LLC’s semantic transport layer that turns choppy, low-bitrate links into something you can actually work over.

From kilobytes to bytes: semantic compression that changes what’s possible.

Typical web and API payloads are chatty – JSON, HTML, images, JavaScript. BullFrog LDC replaces all of that with a compact stream of template IDs, opcodes, and parameters. A 2–10 KB status report can shrink to roughly 20–80 bytes, cutting bandwidth requirements by tens of times and dramatically reducing on-air time.

50–100× smaller Typical JSON or form posts become tiny semantic messages, enabling meaningful communication on links that were previously unusable.
Faster intent delivery Less data on the wire means shorter airtime per message, lower latency, and more chances to get through noisy or intermittent channels.
Bring your own link If your modem or radio shows up as a Linux network interface and accepts ip routes, BullFrog LDC can ride on top of it.
Radio-native by design BullFrog LDC treats any path – HF/VHF/UHF data radios, ham links, SAT terminals, point-to-point microwave, even serial-over-IP and ancient copper – as just another route in Linux. You focus on where messages should go; BullFrog LDC handles how to pack, prioritize, and ship them.
For emergency & field operations

Stay connected on a shoestring link

When a region is cut off, you may be operating on a single shared SAT terminal, an HF data link, or a small UHF radio path. BullFrog LDC lets your FrogNet move:

  • High-priority SOS and status messages that fit into a few dozen bytes.
  • Structured forms (damage, medical, logistics) sent as compressed semantic packets.
  • Command-and-control updates that don’t require full Internet or VPN tunnels.
  • Store-and-forward messaging when links are intermittent or scheduled.
For pioneers & builders

A radio overlay you can understand

BullFrog LDC is implemented with transparent, inspectable tooling. At its core:

  • Runs on Linux – no proprietary black boxes required.
  • Uses standard ip routing: if you can add an ip route to an interface, you can plug it into BullFrog LDC.
  • Plays well with existing radios and modems instead of replacing them.
  • Extensible: add new templates, message types, and integrations without rewriting the transport.

The result: a long-distance digital nerve system that’s simple to reason about, simple to debug, and shockingly frugal with bandwidth.

Under the hood

A composable, transparent architecture.

FrogNet from Fawcett Innovations, LLC is built from simple, well-understood components – Linux, dnsmasq, NetworkManager, Apache, and the custom FrogNet and BullFrog LDC layers – wrapped in an automation system that does what a seasoned network engineer would do by hand.

Building blocks
  • FrogNet Host: A Linux computer (Pi, NUC, or repurposed laptop) that becomes the brain of your network.
  • LillyPad-style routers: Small routers in AP mode; the host claims the .1 address and runs DHCP, DNS, and routing.
  • AI Host Platform: Local language models, retrieval, and Knowledge Packs for specific domains (agriculture, emergency response, industrial, home).
  • BullFrog LDC: The long-distance semantic transport layer that rides over any Linux-routable link.
  • Self-healing directory: Each FrogNet announces its presence and routes; the network converges as nodes join, move, or reconnect.
From upstream dependency to autonomy

Cloud when you have it, independence when you don’t

In a traditional network, your router depends on an upstream ISP for DNS, DHCP, and path decisions; when that link dies, everything goes dark. In a FrogNet from Fawcett Innovations, LLC, the upstream is just another route – useful, but not required.

  • Local name resolution and routing across all joined FrogNets.
  • Automatic detection of upstream links via ip and NetworkManager events.
  • Traffic steering: choose what uses the Internet, what stays local, and what rides BullFrog LDC.
  • Designed for clarity: configuration is scriptable and inspectable, not hidden behind wizards.
